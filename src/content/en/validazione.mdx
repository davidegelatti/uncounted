# Validation

An estimate of traffic you can’t see can’t be compared with reality: reality, after all, is what you can’t see.
What you can do is build a fake site where everything is known, hide the part without consent from the estimator and check whether it finds it.

<section id="how">

## How an estimate is checked

A generator simulates **every single visitor**: they arrive from a channel, accept, decline or ignore the banner, come back a certain number of times, buy or don’t. Two things come out of it:

- an export identical to GA4’s, with only the visits of those who accepted;
- the **right answer**: the total traffic, which the estimator doesn’t see.

The generator works per visitor and the estimator works on totals, with different formulas: if they used the same ones, the test would be circular.
Each scenario is repeated 500 times with different data. What is measured is **how often the true value falls within the stated range**.

</section>

<section id="calibration">

## Calibration: is “8 times out of 10” true?

In these tests the estimator’s assumptions are right on average: the true behaviors are drawn from the same ranges the estimator assumes. Here the coverage **must** hold: about 80 times out of 100 for the “likely” range, 95 for “almost certainly”.
With 500 runs, the margin of the measurement itself is about ±4 points on the first number and ±2 on the second.

## Robustness: what a wrong assumption costs

Here one assumption is **deliberately wrong**. Coverage drops, and that’s unavoidable: no range protects against a false assumption. The point is to know by how much.

<Tabella lingua="en" scenari={['B6', 'B7', 'B8a', 'B8b', 'B9', 'B10a', 'B10b', 'B11a', 'B11b', 'B12a', 'B12b', 'B14', 'B16a', 'B16d', 'B16b', 'B16c']} />

<Tabella lingua="en" tipo="errore" scenari={['B6', 'B7', 'B8a', 'B8b', 'B9', 'B10a', 'B10b', 'B11a', 'B11b', 'B12a', 'B12b', 'B14', 'B16a', 'B16d', 'B16b', 'B16c']} />

What these rows say:

- **Wrong “how well they convert”** (1.0 or 0.15 instead of 0.4): sessions stay correct, transactions are off by 13–20% and the range contains the true value about one time in three. **With real orders the error disappears.**
- **Consent very different across channels**: totals hold, but the session range **for a single channel** contains the true value only 30 times out of 100.
- **Rate declared wrongly**: a “per banner” rate declared “per visitor” inflates sessions by 19%; a rate that excludes visitors who ignore the banner, declared complete, reduces them by 12%. Declared correctly, the estimate is correct and cautious again.
- **Consent differs between mobile and desktop**: with differences like those tested (50% consent on mobile and 75% on desktop, one and a half times the visits on desktop), with the overall rate alone sessions come out about 4% too high, even with the rate declared precise, but coverage changes little (76–77 out of 100 instead of 79); with bigger differences the error grows. With rates by device the error disappears; with a similar number of visits across devices there is no error even without them.
- **Undeclared banner change** and **irregular key events**: no harm to the totals.

</section>

## Ad blockers and real orders

With real orders, the result depends on the share of orders GA4 misses for reasons other than consent, that is, on the audience type you choose.

<Tabella lingua="en" scenari={['B13', 'B13b', 'B15a', 'B15b', 'B15c']} />

<Tabella lingua="en" tipo="errore" scenari={['B13', 'B13b', 'B15a', 'B15b', 'B15c']} />

- **True share 20%, answer “mixed / I don’t know”**: the true value always stays within the “almost certainly” range, but the central estimate of transactions is 14% too high.
- **True share 20%, wrong answer “consumer”**: the estimate is 16% too high and the true value falls outside the range two times out of three. The audience answer matters.
- **Ad blockers that decline more often and partly hide the banner**: with real orders, transactions stay correct. **Sessions** come out slightly below actual traffic (by 2% with 12% of visitors blocking GA, by 9% with 35%): the estimator doesn’t estimate visitors GA4 wouldn’t see even with consent.

</section>

<section id="reading">

## What to take away

1. When the assumptions are reasonable, the ranges keep their promise.
2. **Total sessions** are the most robust estimate; they depend on the consent rate and how it’s measured.
3. **Orders and revenue** depend on a weak assumption (“how well they convert”): real orders replace it, but bring in the audience question.
4. The **channel breakdown** should be read as a pointer, not a measurement.

</section>

<section id="reproduce">

## How to rerun the tests

The generator, scenarios and results are in the publicly available source code, in the <a href="https://github.com/davidegelatti/uncounted" target="_blank" rel="noopener">GitHub repository<span aria-hidden="true"> ↗</span><span class="nascosto-vis"> (opens in a new tab)</span></a>. To rerun the tests, download the repository, install the dependencies with `npm ci` and run `npm run validazione`: it reruns everything from scratch (about half an hour) and regenerates both the tables on this page and the `VALIDAZIONE.md` file. An automated test also checks that the calculation engine always returns the same numbers, digit for digit, for the same data and assumptions.

</section>
